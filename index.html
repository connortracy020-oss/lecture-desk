What happens the first time you open it

The Mac version isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the first time. You approve it once:

  1. Open the downloaded file and drag Lecture Desk into Applications.
  2. Open it from Applications. macOS says it couldn't verify the app. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then enter your Mac's password.Open Privacy & Security
  4. If macOS asks one more time, click Open Anyway. From then on it opens like any other app.

The Mac version doesn't update itself yet, so check back here for new versions. After you install one, macOS may ask again.

What else it does

Recording
Your microphone, the computer's audio for online classes, or both, with a live transcript while class is going.
Ask your classes
Ask about one lecture, one class or all of them. Answers come from your recordings and readings, with links to the passages.
Flashcards
Made from your study notes, each card linked to where it came from. The ones you miss come back sooner.
Read aloud
PDFs and textbooks in a natural voice, highlighting each sentence and remembering where you stopped.
Your schedule
From a screenshot or a PDF. Each class keeps its own lectures, slides and readings.
Export
Notes, practice questions and the timed transcript as a PDF or a web page.
Offline
After a one-time setup, transcription, notes, search and read-aloud all work offline.
Graphics acceleration
Windows laptops with an NVIDIA graphics card can add optional acceleration for much faster notes and transcription. Macs use their built-in graphics automatically.

Your classes stay on your laptop.

Recording, transcription, notes, search and answers all run on your own computer. There's no account to make, no ads, and nothing reports back to me.

The only way your class content leaves your laptop is if you turn on one of the optional cloud features with your own API key, from a provider you pick. Cloud transcription asks before every upload.

Why I built it

I'm an engineering student at Oregon State, and I kept missing things in lecture. Going back to a recording should have fixed that, but finding the one explanation I needed meant scrubbing through an hour of audio, and it took forever.

The AI note tools I found wanted my lectures uploaded to their servers, and I didn't trust that with my classes. So I built Lecture Desk for myself first, to run on my own laptop. It worked well enough that I figured other students could use it too.

Connor

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, with no subscription. The one thing that can cost money is optional cloud AI, which runs on your own API key, so that provider bills you directly if you turn it on.

Am I allowed to record my classes?

That depends on your school and your instructor. Check your class's recording rules and get any permission you need before you record.

How long do notes take?

It depends on your laptop and how long the lecture is. Lecture Desk shows an estimate before it starts and keeps working in the background. An NVIDIA graphics card makes it much faster.

Microsoft Store or the direct installer?

Use the Store if you can: it's signed and updates itself. The direct installer is the same app for laptops without the Store, and you'll need to download new versions from this page.

Is there a Mac version?

Yes, a preview for Macs with Apple silicon. It isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open it you approve it once in System Settings; the steps are in the download section at the top. It doesn't update itself yet.
